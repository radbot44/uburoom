## Real-time playback synchronization

### Timing baseline and clock estimation — 2026-10-16

M1-P5/M1-P6 now have bounded local frame diagnostics and an offline pairwise report. Room polls carry server receive/send timestamps and monotonic processing duration; the playback clock uses the client's monotonic send/receive times, a bounded low-delay filter, at most 2 ms/s refinement, freshness expiry, and reset/discontinuity handling. ### Bounded gentle correction increment

An experimental policy requires two seconds of same-direction meaningful drift before using 1.005× or 0.995×. It is **disabled by default** and explicitly enabled only by the isolated correction comparison runner. Ordinary players retain the existing ±4% gain/150 ms deadband, with the new recovery/visibility guards below. The candidate's base tolerance is 150 ms plus one observed source-frame interval (capped at 100 ms), widened for clock uncertainty up to 250 ms. Entry requires another 50 ms beyond that tolerance; exit uses 60% of the tolerance to avoid threshold chatter. The cadence estimate uses the median of at most 24 contiguous advancing callback media intervals, with at least eight required; sparse callbacks do not establish cadence. Without that estimate, the 150 ms base applies. These are experimental tuning values, not validated perceptual thresholds or a new precision guarantee.

A candidate burst ends after ten seconds at the next synchronization evaluation (normally every 500 ms), followed by at least ten seconds at native speed and fresh persistence evidence. Sign reversal, clock loss, inadequate buffer, pause, waiting, seeking and lifecycle changes stop it. Both policies require visible, playing, non-seeking, non-waiting playback with readyState ≥3, at least one second of forward buffer and a valid clock before nudging. Pause/wait/seek/teardown, offline/pagehide and hiding the document immediately restore 1.00×; hidden documents do not start nudges. The candidate discards old evidence on room/media/stream/timeline changes and long evaluation gaps. Correction stays entirely local. Initial scheduled readiness, independent recovery, per-user tracks and shared commands retain their existing boundaries. The existing >650 ms hard-seek path is unchanged; this increment does not implement frame-quantized seeks, repeated-seek limiting or scheduled ordinary controls.

### Joint M1-P5/M1-P6 human verification

The owner will test the implemented M1-P5 work alongside M1-P6 using this narrow, roughly five-minute viewing check. It remains pending. Use the normal browser/device and a familiar title, with a second viewer or device in the same room. The agent handles setup and technical diagnostics; the owner does not need to measure timings, collect exports, change refresh rates or simulate network faults. Checks on the installed service require a separately approved deployment; this phase does not authorize one. The experimental gentle policy stays disabled while its regression is investigated.

1. **Watch normally for about three minutes.** Does motion look smooth, does dialogue match the picture, and do both viewers seem to be seeing the same moment? Ordinary conversation or comparing a recognizable scene is enough; no stopwatch or frame counting.
2. **Pause, resume and seek once.** Do both viewers respond and settle back into smooth playback together?
3. **Switch away from one viewer's tab for about ten seconds, then return.** Does the other viewer keep playing normally, and does the returning viewer settle back in without persistent stutter or an obvious gap?

“Looks good on all three” is sufficient feedback. Otherwise, describe the step and what looked or sounded wrong. These are subjective acceptance checks for the tested setup, not a request for a certification matrix or proof of millisecond targets.

Technical measurements remain the agent's work: retain revision/environment context and available diagnostics, investigate the candidate's Chrome drops, compare recovery strategies, and assess skew, cadence, audio, transport and network/lifecycle coverage separately. Physical measurements that cannot be automated remain explicitly unverified unless separately arranged; the owner's visual approval does not close those engineering gates.
